## 3. Annotation

The annotation scheme is in `research/annotation/GUIDE.md`.

- **Per excerpt:** whether there is a commitment, and what the right decision is: suggestion, clarification or nothing.
- **Per item:** kind (commitment, request, offer, change, withdrawal, group plan), task, owner, deadline as it was said, kind of agreement, and the identifier of the source utterance.

Of the 39 excerpts, the right decision is a suggestion for 16, clarification for 5, and "no commitment" for 18.

**AMI's definition and ours differ:**
- AMI files agreeing on a meeting time (IB4010) and a conditional offer (IS1008b) under "action" too;
- for us the former is a decision, and the latter needs clarification.

I measured on the **held-out evaluation side** (18 excerpts), against manually verified labels.

| Rule-based | 10/18 | 5 | 1 | 7/12 (+8 extra) | 5/7 · 6/7 · 7/7 | 16 | 0/9 |
| Claude Haiku 5.5 | 13/18 | 2 | 0 | 11/12 (+2 extra) | 11/11 · 8/11 · 9/11 | 8 | – |
| Claude Haiku 5.5 + prefilter | 12/18 | 2 | 0 | 11/12 (+2 extra) | 10/11 · 8/11 · 9/11 | 9 | 0/9 |

On the development side (21 excerpts):
- **rule-based:** right decision 13/21, false suggestions 8, missed 0, 15 extra items, 26 human corrections;
- **Claude Haiku 5.5:** right decision 17/21, false suggestions 0, missed 2, 6 human corrections.

**Haiku is clearly better than the rule-based solution:**
- false suggestions went down from 5 to 2;
- human corrections from 16 to 8;
- it found 11/12 of the commitments, the baseline only 7/12.

**Haiku's errors on the evaluation side:**
- **Needless clarification:** for three control excerpts and for agreeing on a meeting time (IB4010) it asks for clarification when it should not. This is the less harmful error: a question, not a false commitment.
- **Invented owner (hu-04):** from the passive sentence "A jegyzőkönyv holnapra meglesz" ("The minutes will be ready by tomorrow") it makes the speaker the owner. The annotation rule explicitly forbids this.
- **Immediate action (IS1008b):** it suggests a commitment from "I will invite…", said during the meeting.
- **Owner:** among the commitments found, it gets the owner wrong for 3. This is the weakest field.

**With the prefilter:**
- it dropped none of the excerpts that need handling (0/9);
- it filtered out 6 excerpts in all, so 33 requests went out instead of 39.

The difference between the two Haiku rows (13 and 12 right decisions) is not caused by the prefilter. The answer differs for a single excerpt (TS3009b-a1), and both runs received that one: the model does not answer exactly the same way twice.

**Cost:** about 0.03 dollars for the two full runs together.

**Typical errors of the rule-based baseline:**
- **Immediate actions:** it makes suggestions from them ("I will present…", "I will close the meeting", "Most átküldöm a linket", "I'm sending the link now").
- **Past tense, joke, conditional offer:** it also takes a report in the past tense ("elküldtem", "I've sent it"), a joke ("megveszem a kávégépet", "I'll buy the coffee machine") and a conditional offer for commitments.
- **Invented owner:** it reads the answer "Jó kérdés, én most nem érek rá" ("Good question, I don't have time now") as acceptance (hu-03).
- **Missed assignment:** it misses an assignment with no first person ("…are going to work together", TS3005c).

**Keyword prefilter:** it dropped none of the excerpts that need handling (0/21), but of the negative ones it filtered out only 6. Cheap, but it saves little.

**Measurement:** I approximate the task and deadline match by word overlap. `research/results/*_items.csv` lists every pair for manual checking. The sample is small: it shows a direction, but it is not statistically reliable.

## 5. Trial of the business rules

The rules are in `docs/BUSINESS_RULES.md`. In short:

- **Three layers:** the system keeps the conversation, the recognizer's suggestion and the confirmation of the person concerned apart.
- **State machine:** `proposed` → `accepted` → `completed` / `rejected` / `withdrawn`. `overdue` is a derived state, so being late does not rule out completion. `withdrawn` was added after the supervisor's feedback, because a withdrawal is not the same as rejecting a suggestion.
- **Change:** it is created as a new version and does not silently overwrite the commitment. "Inkább hétfőre tudom" ("I can do it by Monday rather") stays a proposal until the requester accepts it; Friday holds until then.
- **Decision table:** says when a suggestion may be made, when clarification has to be asked for, and when no commitment may be created. Every row has an example from the sample.

## 6. Application: manual commitment management

In the offline development version (`SlowsengerOffline`, with data kept in the browser) the whole manual workflow works:

1. **Suggestion:** a "commitment" button on a chat message, which creates a suggestion tied to the identifier of the source message.
2. **Acceptance or rejection:** only the owner can do it. A suggestion with no owner first has to be taken on by someone.
3. **Closing:** completion (late too), or withdrawal with a mandatory reason.
4. **Deadline change:** accepted by the requester or the owner, as in point 3.
5. **Log:** every transition goes into the commitment's log.

**Testing:**
- the state machine runs with 12 unit tests;
- the application runs 117 tests in all;
- a walk-through in the browser checks: message → suggestion → acceptance → change → withdrawal → log → reload.

**Not there yet:**
- **Room and membership:** there is no shared room or membership. For now the source message identifier points to the user's own copy of the message, because of the current sender-side and recipient-side model.
- **Server-side enforcement:** the state machine now runs in the client. In the Supabase version database functions and RLS have to enforce it, so that the client cannot get around it.
- **Connecting the recognizer:** the automatic recognizer is not connected to the application yet.
